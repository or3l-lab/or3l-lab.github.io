02The challenge

A machine or a line can run for years with incomplete, outdated or even missing documentation. Repairing, changing or replacing it then becomes risky.

  • Understand what the equipment really does, not what we assume it does.
  • Recover the missing information: inputs/outputs, sequences, parameters, timings.
  • Decide with full knowledge: repair, make reliable, adapt or replace.

Typical situations

01

Missing or outdated documentation

Drawings no longer exist, or do not reflect the installed equipment.

02

Obsolete components

A part, PLC or drive is no longer made: an equivalent must be found without changing the behaviour.

03

Program or parameters not found

The program was never backed up, or its author has left. Without it, replacing hardware becomes a gamble.

04

Unrecorded changes

Over successive repairs the equipment was modified and the documentation did not follow.

04What is captured

Depending on the equipment, several sources cross-check one another. None is enough alone: comparing them is what produces a reliable model.

Signals and I/O

Inputs and outputs identified and recorded as timing diagrams: you see in which order things happen.

Electrical drawings

The actual wiring is traced and compared with the existing drawings, exposing unrecorded changes.

Program and parameters

Program structure, variables and timers; drive and device parameters.

Networks and communication

Devices present, exchanges and addresses: who talks to whom, and with what data.

Operator interfaces

Screens, alarms and setpoints: what the operator sees often reveals the intended logic.

Mechanics and parts

References, dimensions and equivalents: the basis for replacing a component without changing behaviour.

05Deliverables

The result is equipment that can be repaired, changed or replaced without guessing.

  1. Up-to-date I/O list

    Each signal with its address, role and rest state.

  2. Updated drawings

    The real wiring, marked up and matching the installation.

  3. Functional analysis

    States, sequences, transition conditions and timers, clearly described.

  4. Commented, backed-up program

    A readable, dated, archived version with its history.

  5. Variable dictionary

    A name, a role and a unit for every useful variable.

  6. Maintenance and equivalents file

    Part references, possible equivalents and replacement procedures.

  7. Modernisation plan

    The options for replacing or upgrading, with their risks and priorities.

06Safeguards

Understanding existing equipment means not breaking it. Four rules frame the approach.

Safety first

Read-only by default, lockout before any intervention, site procedures respected.

Back up before acting

A dated, verified copy of the program and parameters before any change.

Validate off-production

Test on a bench or in simulation before touching equipment in service.

Legal and contractual framework

Licences, intellectual property, confidentiality and contracts: check what is allowed before capturing or modifying anything.
